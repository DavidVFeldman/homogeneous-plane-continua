# Homogeneous plane continua in Lean

This repository is a Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of the classification of homogeneous plane
continua (L. C. Hoehn and L. G. Oversteegen, *A complete classification of homogeneous plane
continua*, Acta Math. 216 (2016), 177–216), together with the results from earlier papers that its
proof uses: Bing 1948 and 1951, Bing–Jones 1959, Jones 1941–1975, Hagopian 1975, 1976 and 1984,
Oversteegen–Tymchatyn 1982, Cook 1967, Effros 1965 (by van Mill's proof), Moore 1916–1925, and
Domokos–Prajs 2022, among others. The two final theorems are in `HPC/BJ59/BJ59E.lean`
(namespace `HPC.BJ59`; `Pt` is the Euclidean plane):

```lean
theorem ho16_classification₄ (M : Set Pt) [CompactSpace M] [ConnectedSpace M]
    (hnt : (univ : Set M).Nontrivial) :
    IsHomogeneous M ↔ Nonempty (M ≃ₜ Circle) ∨ IsPseudoArc (univ : Set M) ∨ IsCircleOfPA M

theorem ho16_classification_plane₄ :
    ∃ C₁ C₂ C₃ : Set Pt,
      (Nonempty (C₁ ≃ₜ Circle) ∧ IsPseudoArc C₂ ∧ IsCircleOfPA C₃) ∧
      (IsHomogeneous C₁ ∧ IsHomogeneous C₂ ∧ IsHomogeneous C₃) ∧
      (IsEmpty (C₁ ≃ₜ C₂) ∧ IsEmpty (C₁ ≃ₜ C₃) ∧ IsEmpty (C₂ ≃ₜ C₃)) ∧
      ∀ M : Set Pt, IsCompact M → IsConnected M → M.Nontrivial →
        (IsHomogeneous M ↔ Nonempty (M ≃ₜ C₁) ∨ Nonempty (M ≃ₜ C₂) ∨ Nonempty (M ≃ₜ C₃))
```

`ho16_classification₄`: a nondegenerate plane continuum is homogeneous if and only if it is a
simple closed curve, a pseudo-arc or a circle of pseudo-arcs.

`ho16_classification_plane₄`: the plane contains a simple closed curve, a pseudo-arc and a circle of
pseudo-arcs, each homogeneous and no two homeomorphic, and every nondegenerate homogeneous plane
continuum is homeomorphic to one of the three.

## Status

* No `sorry`, `admit` or `axiom` declaration occurs, outside comments and string literals, in the
  Lean files of the four libraries (`HPC` 709 modules, `HPCExtra` 125, `HPCArchive` 25,
  `HPCCensus` 2, each with its root file). The statement skeletons in `doc/batches/skeletons/`
  contain `sorry`; they belong to no library.
* `axioms.txt` (4030 lines, 4016 entries) is the output of `Axioms.lean`, which runs
  `#print axioms` on theorems of `HPC` and `HPCExtra`. Every entry depends on `propext`,
  `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` or on fewer of them; no other axiom occurs. `Axioms.lean`
  now has five more lines (Bing 1951 Theorem 6, `HPC/Plane/Theorem6Direct.lean`), whose output is
  not yet in `axioms.txt`.
* `native_decide`, and with it `Lean.ofReduceBool`, occurs only in `HPCCensus`
  (`HPC/Census/Census87Checks.lean`), whose theorems no other library imports and `Axioms.lean`
  does not audit.
* The constants on which the two final theorems depend were sent once more to Lean's kernel, in an
  environment built from Mathlib alone, and every one was accepted (`doc/depcensus/replay.txt`).
  The statements of the two theorems, elaborated from `HPC/BJ59/BJ59E.lean` in that environment,
  agree with the replayed types.

## Building

The toolchain is `leanprover/lean4:v4.28.0` (`lean-toolchain`); Mathlib is pinned at tag
`v4.28.0`, commit `8f9d9cff6bd728b17a24e163c9402775d9e6a365` (`lake-manifest.json`). From the root:

```
lake exe cache get                         # Mathlib's compiled files
lake build                                 # the library HPC (the classification)
lake build HPCExtra HPCArchive HPCCensus   # the other three libraries
lake env lean Axioms.lean > axioms.txt     # the axiom audit (imports HPC and HPCExtra)
```

Build times have not been measured for this layout of the libraries yet.

## Layout

The four libraries of `lakefile.toml`:

* **`HPC`** (`HPC.lean`, the default target): the classification, that is, the import closure of
  `HPC.BJ59.BJ59E`, 709 modules.
* **`HPCExtra`** (`HPCExtra.lean`, generated by `scripts/split.py`): the modules under `HPC/` outside
  the classification's import closure and outside `HPC/Archive` and `HPC/Census`, 125 modules:
  working files of the Theorem 3–4 campaign (`HPC/Fold`), non-vacuity and certificate files, the band
  and cube models of `HPC/Plane`, a direct proof of Bing 1951 Theorem 6, and counterexamples cited by
  the ledgers.
* **`HPCArchive`** (`HPCArchive.lean`): exploratory files no theorem of the other libraries uses
  (`HPC/Archive`).
* **`HPCCensus`** (`HPCCensus.lean`): `native_decide` checks of finite combinatorics (`HPC/Census`).

## What the classification uses

The two final theorems depend on 17643 constants: 12480 of this repository and 5163 of Mathlib,
Batteries and Lean's core library. Of the 11697 declarations written in the repository, 4689
(40.1 %) are among them, in 446 of the 863 modules counted. The rest is the formalization of the
other theorems of the papers, the campaigns that preceded the route finally taken, and the
counterexamples; `doc/depcensus/` has the files, the definitions and the tables.

## The published proofs

`doc/defects/` records what the formalization found in the published proofs: a ledger ordered by
source and the same defects ordered by severity. It counts 23 printed statements that are false and
18 printed proofs that are defective while the statement is true. The severity document puts two
items first. The step from Jones's decomposition to the circle of pseudo-arcs is used without proof
in Hoehn and Oversteegen's Theorem 2; the first proof of it we found is Theorem 6.1 of Domokos and
Prajs's 2022 preprint (arXiv:2203.15906), formalized here. And Bing and Jones's sketch of a plane
circle of pseudo-arcs (1959, §7) leaves steps unconstructed; the plane example used here
(`HPC.BJ59.bjPlaneExample`) is built by another construction.

## Records

The commissions and reports of the project, batch by batch, are in `doc/batches/`; `LEDGER.md`
records the outcome for each paper and `OPEN.md` the items left open; `doc/notes/` holds
dictionaries and routes for individual papers, `doc/exposition/` expositions of parts of the proof,
and `doc/HISTORY.md` the batch-by-batch record that this file kept until batch 203. The lecture
notes in `doc/lecture-notes/` are eight volumes of LaTeX that follow the formal proof (see
`doc/lecture-notes/README.md`).
