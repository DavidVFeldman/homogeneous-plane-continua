## Items

### 1. Lemma 3 (`isTerminalSub_of_union_terminal`)

The proof follows Fugate's, split into two lemmas.

* `fugate69_lemma3_half` covers the case where `X` is not terminal, with `K = X ∪ Y` terminal and
  `Y ≠ K`. It produces:
  * a continuum `R ⊇ X` that is irreducible from no point of `X`;
  * points `q ∈ Y ∖ X` and `p ∈ R ∖ K` with `K ∪ R` irreducible from `q` to `p`.
* `fugate69_lemma3_core` is Fugate's final paragraph:
  * `L ⊊ R` contains `y` and `p`;
  * `q ∉ L`;
  * `L ∪ K = R ∪ K`, so `x ∈ L`;
  * `L ∩ (S ∪ K)` is a proper subcontinuum of `S ∪ K` that contains `x` and `y`.

The main proof applies the first lemma to `(A, B)` and to `(B, A)`. In each of the two cases of the
terminality of `K` (`R ⊆ K ∪ S` or `S ⊆ R ∪ K`) it then applies the second lemma, with the roles of
the two halves exchanged in one of them.

### 2. Inductiveness and Lemma 4 (HD)

* **`isTerminalSub_iInter`.** This replaces Fugate's argument with points `d` and `e`. For each `n`
  one of the two alternatives of Definition 4 holds. If the first holds for every `n`, take the
  intersection. Otherwise the second holds at some `n₀`, and then, because the sequence decreases,
  it holds for every `n`.
* **`exists_terminal_point_of_isTerminalSub`.** Apply Brouwer reduction (`exists_minimal_of_seq`)
  to "terminal in `M`". If the minimal continuum were nondegenerate, it would decompose
  (hereditary decomposability), and Lemma 3 would give a smaller terminal continuum. So it is a
  point, and that point satisfies condition B (`isTerminalSub_singleton_iff`).

### 3. The halves (`halves_terminal`)

This is the route given in the commission:

* `closure V` is a continuum;
* `A ∪ C = univ`;
* `A ∩ C ⊆ g'`, because `U` is open and disjoint from `V`;
* terminality follows from `inter_isTerminalSub_of_union`, applied once in each order;
* nonemptiness of `A ∩ C` follows from the unicoherence of the space.

### 4. Claim 1 (`claim1_contains_inter`)

Fugate's map `f` is replaced as follows. Fix `p ∈ (A ∩ C) ∖ Q` and `t ∈ Q ∖ (A ∩ C)`. Then `t ∈ V`,
because `cl V ⊆ V ∪ g'`.

* **The lemma used to choose `s`** is `exists_nbhd_not_separated`. It gives a neighbourhood `O` of
  `p` such that `g_t` separates `g'` from the element of no point of `O`:
  * if `g_t` is an end element, take `O = S`;
  * otherwise take `O` to be the complement of the closed side `V_t ∪ g_t` of `g_t` that does not
    contain `g'`.
* **Choice of `s`.** Since `p ∈ cl V`, there is a point `s ∈ V ∩ O ∖ (Q ∪ g_t)`. Theorem 8 (a)
  (`bingElt_separates_triple`) is applied to `g'`, `g_t` and `g_s`:
  * `g'` cannot separate `g_t` from `g_s`, because both lie in the connected set `V`;
  * `g_t` cannot separate `g'` from `g_s`, by the choice of `O`;
  * so `g_s` separates `g'` from `g_t`. In particular `g_s` is not an end element, and it has sides
    `U_s ⊇ g'` and `V_s ∋ t` (`sides_of_separation`).
* **The continuum `W(s)`.** Fugate's `Cl(B − A) ∩ f⁻¹[½, f(s)]` is `W = (U_s ∪ g_s) ∩ cl V`.
  * `V_s ⊆ V`, so `(U_s ∪ g_s) ∪ cl V` is the whole space.
  * `W` is therefore a continuum by unicoherence. This is the commission's second option,
    "`W(s) = closure V ∩ (U_s ∪ g_s)`", and Nadler 6.3 is not needed.
* **The contradiction** (`claim1_core`) is Fugate's:
  * `W` meets `A ∩ C` at `p`;
  * `s ∈ W ∖ (A ∩ C ∪ Q)`;
  * `t ∈ Q ∖ (A ∩ C ∪ W)`;
  * so `A ∩ C` is not terminal in `C`.

### 5. `exists_mu`

Lemma 4 (HD), applied to `A ∩ C` in `A`, gives `μ ∈ A ∩ C` with `CondB A μ`. To prove `CondB C μ`,
take subcontinua `H` and `K` of `C` containing `μ`, neither contained in the other. If both lie in
`A`, this contradicts `CondB A μ`. Otherwise, say `H ⊄ A`:

* by Claim 1, `A ∩ C ⊆ H`;
* hence `K ⊄ A ∩ C`, and Claim 1 again gives `A ∩ C ⊆ K`;
* now `H`, `K` and `A` form a triod (`isTriod_union_of_three`), which contradicts atriodicity.

### 6. Claim 2 and the chain

* **`claim2`.** This is Fugate's Claim 2, stated for preconnected sets so that it can feed
  `exists_closed_split_of_no_connected`; the proof passes to closures.
  * `N ∩ C` is a continuum, by hereditary unicoherence of `M`.
  * It meets `A ∩ C`, because `N` is connected and `M = A ∪ C`.
  * It is not contained in `A`.
  * So by Claim 1 it contains `μ`, which lies in `U`, a contradiction.
* **`epsCoverable_of_halves`.**
  * Moore's separation (`exists_closed_split_of_no_connected`) gives the split of `M − U`.
  * `normal_separation` gives the open sets `S` and `T`.
  * The weak chain is
    `(E_i ∩ S) − cl U` for `i ≤ x − 1`, then `E_x ∩ S`, then `(F_y ∩ T) ∪ U`, then `F_j ∩ T` for
    `j = y − 1, …, 1`.
  * Removing `cl U` from links `E_i` with `i ≤ x − 2` changes nothing, since those links miss
    `E_x ⊇ cl U`.
  * The weak chain is trimmed to a chain with `exists_chain_of_weak` (batch 96).
  * Coverage: a point of `cl U` that lies in `E_{x−1}` is covered by `E_x ∩ S`.

### 7. Theorem 11

* **The minimal counterexample** `S'` comes from `exists_minimal_notCoverable`, and `S'` satisfies
  `BingS` (`bingS_subcontinuum`).
* **An interior element** comes from `exists_not_endElt`. There are two distinct end elements
  (`exists_two_endElts`). Their union is closed with empty interior
  (`interior_union_isClosed_of_interior_empty`), so a third element exists. Of three distinct
  elements, one separates the other two (Theorem 8 (a)), so that one is not an end element.
* **Assembling the proof:**
  * the sides of the interior element come from `exists_two_sides` and `sides_continua`;
  * `exists_mu` gives `μ`;
  * the halves are proper subcontinua of `S'`, so they are `ε`-coverable by minimality;
  * `fugate_lemma1`, applied to `{μ}` (condition B, `isTerminalSub_singleton_iff`), refines those
    chains so that `μ` lies in the last link;
  * `epsCoverable_of_halves` then covers `S'`, a contradiction.
* **Transfer from `S'` to `S`.** Condition B and Claim 1 pass from the subtype `S'` to `S` via
  `condB_image_val` and `claim1_image_val`.
* **Necessity** comes from batch 96 (`isAtriodic_of_isSnakeLike`,
  `isHereditarilyUnicoherent_of_isSnakeLike`).

## Ledger: Fugate 1969, Case II (and Theorem 11)

What the formal proof needed beyond Fugate's text:

1. **Lemma 3, "Clearly `A ⊆ R`".** The commission's gloss works. Replacing `R` with `R ∪ A` keeps
   (†), and `R ∪ A` still meets `A`.
2. **Lemma 3, "points `p ∈ R` and `q ∈ K`".** Lemma 2 only gives `K ∪ R` irreducible between a
   point `q` of `K` and some point `p`. Getting `p ∈ R ∖ K` requires `R ⊄ K`, which Fugate does not
   argue. It is proved from Sorgenfrey's theorem:
   * an irreducible pair `r₁, r₂` of `R` avoids `A`, by (†);
   * if `R ⊆ K`, the pair lies in `B`, so `R ∩ B = R` by hereditary unicoherence and
     irreducibility;
   * that gives `A ⊆ R ⊆ B` and `K = B`, a contradiction.

   Without this, the case `R ⊆ K` is a real gap in the text.
3. **Lemma 3, the "similar" case `R ⊆ S ∪ K`.** It is the mirror image and uses the same core
   lemma. Fugate's remark "`q ∈ B − (L ∩ (S ∪ K)) ⊆ S`" is not needed: properness only needs
   `q ∈ K ⊆ S ∪ K` and `q ∉ L`.
4. **Lemma 3, "`L ∩ (S ∪ K)` is a continuum".** This is hereditary unicoherence, unstated by
   Fugate (`isContinuum_inter_of_hu`).
5. **Lemma 4, inductiveness.** A two-line argument by monotonicity replaces Fugate's.
6. **Claim 1, the choice of `s`** (the step "there is `s ∈ V ∩ f⁻¹(0, f(t)) ∩ (B − A)`"). With
   elements instead of `f`, this needs `exists_nbhd_not_separated` together with Theorem 8 (a). Upper
   semicontinuity (`bingElt_usc`) is not used.
7. **Claim 1, `W(s)` is a continuum.** This follows from unicoherence of the space, using the sides
   of `g_s` and `V_s ⊆ V`.
8. **"`K` is a terminal subcontinuum of `Cl(B − A)`".** Two points needed attention:
   * the triod `R ∪ L ∪ A` needs `A ⊄ R ∪ L`, which holds because `U ≠ ∅` and `U ∩ cl V = ∅`;
   * the hereditary unicoherence hypothesis of `isTriod_union_of_three` is `BingS`.
9. **Claim 2, "`N` must intersect `A ∩ Cl(B − A)`".** This uses the connectedness of `N` and
   `M = A ∪ C`. "`N ∩ Cl(B − A)` is a subcontinuum" uses hereditary unicoherence of `M`.
10. **The displayed chain.** Fugate's chain is in general only a weak chain: links may be empty, and
    `E_x ∩ S` need not meet `(F_y ∩ T) ∪ U`. It is trimmed (`exists_chain_of_weak`). The mesh is
    `ε`, because `(F_y ∩ T) ∪ U ⊆ F_y`.
11. **Bing's Theorem 11 along Fugate's lines (commission §0).**
    * Bing's `K` becomes `C = Cl(B − A)`, and his `p'` becomes `μ`, a terminal point of both
      halves.
    * Bing's separation of `S − N` is Claim 2 (with Moore's theorem 44).
    * Bing's displayed chain is the trimmed weak chain above.
    * Theorem 10 is not used. Neither is the degenerate interior element refuted in batch 96
      (`theorem11_hdeg_refuted`). What replaces them is Lemma 4 (HD) and Lemma 1 (Fugate 1966).
