## Cook 1967, Theorem 4 (p. 243)

H. Cook, *Continua which admit only the identity mapping onto non-degenerate subcontinua*, Fund.
Math. 60 (1967), 241–249 (`doc/sources/cook1967.pdf`, PDF page 2, right half). Checked against the
page image in batch 191; it agrees with the transcription of commission 191 §3:

> THEOREM 4. *If f is a mapping of the compact metric continuum X onto the hereditarily
> indecomposable continuum Y, then f is confluent.*
>
> Proof. Suppose that Q is a subcontinuum of Y and C is a component of f⁻¹(Q). Let C₁, C₂, C₃, ...
> be a sequence of subcontinua of X such that (1) for each n, Cₙ₊₁ is a subcontinuum of Cₙ; (2)
> for each n, C is a proper subcontinuum of Cₙ; and (3) C is the common part of continua of that
> sequence. Then, for each n, f(Cₙ) contains a point not in Q and intersects Q; hence, f(Cₙ)
> contains Q. The common part of f(C₁), f(C₂), ... is f(C) and contains Q, but Q contains f(C).
> Then f(C) = Q.

## An account of O–T §2 for batch 192 (pp. 130–136)

Notation of O–T: `S` is a simple closed curve or an arc, `I = [0, 1]`, `π₁` the first coordinate,
`B_t` the segment from `f(t)` to `g(t)`, `T⁰` the vertex set of a graph `T`.

* **Lemma 2.1 (p. 131).** For a light piecewise linear map `f : S → R²` of a simple closed curve
  into the closed unit disk, with `T = f(S)`, the parity `θ(z)` of `#f⁻¹(A ∩ T)`, for a polygonal
  arc `A` from `(2, 0)` to `z ∉ T` with `A⁰ ∩ T = φ`, is well defined. (Proof by induction on the
  number of bounded complementary components of `T`.) The repository has this in ray-crossing
  form: `parity`, `parity_locallyConstant`, `parity_eq_of_isPreconnected`, `parity_far`
  (`HPC/Plane/Jct1.lean`), for closed polygons that need not be simple.
* **Lemma 2.2 (pp. 132–133).** For light PL `f, g : I → R²`, `S_t = B₀ ∪ f[0,t] ∪ g[0,t] ∪ B_t`
  and `U_t = {x ∉ S_t | θ_t(x) = 1}`: if `tᵢ → t` and `x ∉ S_t`, then `x ∈ U_t` iff `x ∈ U_{tᵢ}`
  for all large `i`. (Proof: an arc to the unbounded domain missing `B_t` misses a neighbourhood of
  `B_t`, and `S_{tᵢ}`, `S_t` agree off that neighbourhood.)
* **Lemma 2.3 (pp. 133–134).** For `a < b`, `ε > 0` and light PL `f, g : I → [a,b] × R` with (1)
  `f⁻¹π₁⁻¹(a) = {0} = g⁻¹π₁⁻¹(a)` and `f⁻¹π₁⁻¹(b) = {1} = g⁻¹π₁⁻¹(b)`, (2) `d(f(x), g(x)) < ε`,
  (3) `f(x) ≠ g(y)` for `0 < x < 1` or `0 < y < 1`, and a map `h : I → U ∪ B₀ ∪ B₁` (`U` the
  component of `U₁` whose boundary meets `f(I)` and `g(I)` in nondegenerate continua), the set
  `X = {(x, y) ∈ I² | d(B_x, h(y)) = 0}` contains a continuum meeting `I × {0}` and `I × {1}`; if
  `π₁ h(I) = [a, b]`, also a continuum meeting `{0} × I` and `{1} × I`. The proof is the
  intermediate value argument for `φ(x, y) = ±d(B_x, h(y))` (sign `−` on `U_x ∪ {a} × R`), whose
  continuity is the content (Case 2 uses Lemma 2.1 on small boxes), followed by the "continuum in
  the zero set of a function changing sign" lemma (unstated in O–T).
* **Lemma 2.4 and Remark 2.5 (pp. 134–135).** `σ(h, k) = inf d(h(c), k(c))`; `σ(X)` (resp.
  `σ₀(X)`) is the sup of `σ(h, k)` over continua `C` and maps with `h(C) = k(C)` (resp.
  `h(C) ⊇ k(C)`). If `X` is locally connected, the maps of continua can be replaced by maps of `I`
  losing at most `δ`, hitting prescribed finite sets (chain of small arcs `Iₚ`, `Jₚ` through a
  finite `δ/2`-net of `C`). Remark 2.5: useful when `X` sits in a locally connected `Y` in a small
  neighbourhood of `X`. (Not argued in O–T.)
* **Theorem 2.6 (p. 135).** In an ε-strip with (1)–(3) of Lemma 2.3, every continuum
  `X ⊆ U ∪ B₀ ∪ B₁` with `π₁(X) = [a, b]` has `σ₀*(X) < ε`. Proof: by Remark 2.5 it suffices to
  bound `σ(h, k)` for maps `h, k : I → U ∪ B₀ ∪ B₁` with `π₁ h(I) = [a, b]`; Lemma 2.3 gives
  continua `K ⊆ {d(B_x, h(y)) = 0}` from `I × {0}` to `I × {1}` and
  `L ⊆ {d(B_x, k(y)) = 0}` from `{0} × I` to `{1} × I`; they meet, and at a common point
  `d(h(y), k(y)) ≤ ε₁ = max d(f, g) < ε`.
* **Theorem 2.7 (pp. 135–136).** The same with (1) only at the left end `a` and the condition
  `a ∈ π₁(X)` instead of `π₁(X) = [a, b]`, and the conclusion `σ*(X) < ε` (surjective span). The
  proof uses the sign condition `φ(I × {p}) ≤ 0` when `h(p) ∈ {a} × R`, for both `h` and `k`
  (points `p`, `q` with `h(p), k(q)` on the left side), and the two continua `H`, `K` meet.

**What batch 192 needs to prove `OTThm32`.** Theorem 3.2 is proved "as in Theorem 3.1 except that
we use Theorem 2.7 instead of Theorem 2.6". So batch 192 needs:
1. Theorem 2.7, i.e. Lemma 2.3 in its one-sided form, hence the continuity of `φ` (Lemma 2.2 and
   the parity of Lemma 2.1 for the curve `S_t`), and the zero-set continuum lemma;
2. a reduction from `σ*` of the continuum `h_λ₂(X)` to maps of an arc into a strip neighbourhood
   (Lemma 2.4 / Remark 2.5): in `OTThm32` the surjective span is given by a continuum
   `Z ⊆ A × A` off the diagonal (`SurjSpanPos`), so what is needed is a pair of maps `h, k` from a
   continuum onto `A` with `d(h, k) ≥ α`, which `Z` with its two projections already is; but
   Theorem 2.7 is proved only for maps of `I` (Lemma 2.3 uses `h : I → …`), so either Lemma 2.3 is
   generalized to maps of a continuum (the zero-set argument works on `C × I`, but the
   intermediate value step needs a path `y ↦ (x, y)`), or `A` is approximated by locally
   connected continua in the strip (Remark 2.5, unargued);
3. the strip itself: the PL arcs `f = h̃_λ₁|A₁`, `g = h̃_λ₃|A₁` bounding an ε-strip that contains
   `h_λ₂(X)`. In `OTThm32` the images lie in the disjoint crossing continua `K i`, and the order of
   three of them along the left side (§7, `crossing_traces_separated`) supplies "`h_λ₂(X)`
   separates `h_λ₁(X)` from `h_λ₃(X)`" in the form needed. The PL approximation of the outer two
   continua by arcs disjoint from the middle one (O–T: "Without loss of generality ...") is
   unargued and is the main construction;
4. the counting: choose one `n` with uncountably many indices having span witness `≥ 1/n` and
   gap `π₁ h(y) − max π₁ h(X) > 1/n`, then cluster the maps within `1/(8n)` (commission 191 §9.4);
   three indices in the cluster, ordered by §7.

**Doubts about the claim.** None about `OTThm32` itself (it adds to the printed Theorem 3.2 the
hypothesis that the images lie in disjoint crossing continua, which the use in Corollary 3.5
supplies). The printed Theorem 3.2 without that hypothesis is not decided: its proof needs a
separating triple and the single-point supposition, neither argued.

## The route of batch 192 (commission 192 §3–§7)

`OTThm32` is proved (`ot82_theorem32`), and with it `theoremC : TheoremC`, by the following route,
which uses the ray-crossing `parity` of closed polygons (not necessarily simple) in place of O–T's
Lemma 2.1 and needs no Jordan curve theorem, no continuum in a zero set and no locally connected
neighbourhood. **§3 (`OT82Parity.lean`):** the parity vanishes off a compact convex set containing
the polygon (separation by a half-plane), is unchanged under moving the vertices by less than the
distance to the polygon (difference of the two crossing counts written as a sum of quadrilateral
parities, each vanishing by the convex case), and flips across a vertical edge with no other edge
nearby. **§4 (`OT82Zero.lean`):** two continuous functions on `[0, 1] × [p, q]` with the sign
pattern of O–T's `φ` (for `h`) and `ψ` (for `k`) have a common zero, by a continuous argument of
`φ + iψ` and the winding count of its four sides. **§5 (`OT82Strip.lean`):** for piecewise linear
paths `f = plPath a`, `g = plPath b` and the closed polygons `S_t = stripPoly a b t`, the parity at a
point off `S_{t₀}` is locally constant in `(t, z)` (vertex perturbation), so the predicate
`StripInner` (on `x = 0`, or inside `S_t`) is locally constant off `f`, `g` and `B_{t₀}`, and the
sign function `stripSign = ∓ d(B_t, h(u))` is continuous; a continuum `W` in the strip, meeting
`x = 0` only inside `B₀` and missing `f`, `g`, `B₁`, is inside `S_1` (connectedness); for a span
witness `Z ⊆ W × W` a fine chain in `Z` joined by segments gives paths `h`, `k` in a thin
neighbourhood of `W`, the two sign functions satisfy §4, and at the common zero `h(u), k(u)` both
lie on one `B_t`, of length `≤ ε`: the strip lemma `strip_lemma`. **§6 (`OT82Order.lean`):** two
disjoint continua crossing `[a, b] × ℝ` have strictly ordered traces on `x = a`
(`crossing_traces_separated`): a polygonal path in a thin neighbourhood of one continuum, closed up
outside the strip, would otherwise give a polygon whose parity differs at two points joined inside
the other continuum. **§7 (`OT82B.lean`):** if uncountably many images had positive surjective
span, one `n` serves uncountably many indices for both the span witness and the gap, a
separability argument clusters uncountably many maps within `3ρ` (`ρ = 1/(4(n+1))`), §6 orders
three of them, and the strip lemma for the middle image between paths through the images of a fine
chain of `Y` under the outer two maps gives a pair at distance `≤ 3ρ < 1/(n+1)`, a contradiction.
`theoremC := theoremC_of_thm32 ot82_theorem32`.
