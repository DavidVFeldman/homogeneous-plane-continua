# Part G: the circle of pseudo-arcs (Bing–Jones 1959)

State of the repository as of batch 198. Source: R. H. Bing and F. B. Jones, *Another homogeneous
plane continuum*, Trans. AMS 90 (1959), 171–192 (`doc/sources/bing-jones1959.pdf`). Lean files:
`HPC/BJ59/` (definitions and named inputs in `Defs.lean`, the commission's theorems in
`BJ59A.lean`, non-vacuity in `NonVac198.lean`).

## The plan

Part F (batch 197) settled the indecomposable case of the classification of homogeneous plane
continua: such a continuum is a pseudo-arc (`ho16_indec_homogeneous_iff`). For the decomposable
case, Jones 1955 Theorem 2 (`jones55_theorem2'`) gives a continuous decomposition over a circle whose
elements are homogeneous, indecomposable and non-separating; by Part F each non-degenerate element
is a pseudo-arc (`isPseudoArc_Lset`, proved in batch 198, unconditionally). Three pieces are missing
and are recorded as named `Prop`s in `HPC/BJ59/Defs.lean`:

1. `BJThm10`: Bing–Jones Theorem 10 (pp. 181–182) in fibre-preserving form, for continuous
   snake-like arcs of pseudo-arcs;
2. `HOCircleLike`: a decomposable homogeneous plane continuum is circle-like (not in Bing–Jones;
   Prajs 2000 shows a continuous decomposition into pseudo-arcs over a circle is not enough);
3. `BJPlaneExample`: some plane continuum is a circle of pseudo-arcs (Bing–Jones §7, a sketch).

## What batch 198 proved

**Top down.** From the three named inputs, the classification in both directions:
`ho16_classification` (from `BJThm10`, `HOCircleLike`): a non-degenerate plane continuum is
homogeneous iff it is a simple closed curve, a pseudo-arc or a circle of pseudo-arcs; and
`ho16_classification_plane` (from all three): three plane continua, each homogeneous, pairwise
non-homeomorphic, and every non-degenerate plane continuum is homogeneous iff it is homeomorphic to
one of them. On the way: the halves of a circle of pseudo-arcs are continuous snake-like arcs of
pseudo-arcs (`HPC/BJ59/Halves.lean`, using Nadler 12.51 from batch 132), gluing two applications of
`BJThm10` (`HPC/BJ59/Glue.lean`) gives `homeomorph_of_isCircleOfPA` and
`isHomogeneous_of_isCircleOfPA`; the quotient map of a continuous decomposition is open
(`isOpenMap_quotient_mk_of_continuous`); the decomposable case
`circle_or_circleOfPA_of_decomposable` (from `HOCircleLike`); and the distinctness facts (the circle
and a circle of pseudo-arcs are decomposable, the pseudo-arc is not; no proper subset of the circle is
a pseudo-arc).

**Bottom up.** Bing–Jones §§3–4: Theorem 1 is in the library (`isUnicoherent_of_isSnakeLike`);
Theorem 2 (`bj_theorem2`, with the missing step about `d_t` supplied), Theorem 3 (`bj_theorem3`,
with the printed chain repaired at both junctions), Theorem 4 (`bj_theorem4`), Theorem 6
(`bj_theorem6`, on indices) are proved. Theorem 5 is false as printed (`bj_theorem5_printed_false`,
a degenerate certificate; a non-degenerate construction in prose, `LEDGER.md` BJ.5) and proved in
the commission's repaired form (`bj_theorem5`, with `f` open and no fibre meeting a link only inside
`Ū`).

## Batch 202: Part G, V — a plane circle of pseudo-arcs (§7)

Bing–Jones §7 asserts chains `D₁, D₂, ⋯` with (1)–(9) without constructing them (`LEDGER.md`
BJ.41–BJ.47). The example is built here by a different route (commission 202 §1), in four parts.

**Circular descriptions and the recognition theorem** (`CircDescr`, `HPC/BJ59/CircDescr.lean`;
`HPC/BJ59/CircFibre.lean`; `CircDescr.isCircleOfPA`, `HPC/BJ59/CircMap.lean`). Nested circular
chains refining along lifted patterns of degree one, each link carrying an arc of `ℝ/ℤ` of length
`λ_k` centred at `o_k + t/N_k`. The runs of `y` (links whose arcs contain `y`) form chains; a run of
level `k + 1` is crooked in the run of level `k`, so the fibres (intersections of the runs) are
pseudo-arcs; survival makes the map `fmap : M → Circle` open; the trace chains make `M`
circle-like. `M` is then a circle of pseudo-arcs through `isCircleOfPA_iff`.

**Sweep patterns** (`HPC/BJ59/Sweep.lean`, `HPC/BJ59/SweepPattern.lean`). The sweep lemma is stated
for blocks of a fixed length `L` with windows `b n`, `t n` and alternating directions, and allows
at most two events in the interval (the pattern needs no more: an interval of length `P + 3`
contains at most two multiples of `P = 3L`). `sweepX m S` is the pattern of commission §4.2;
`exists_sweepPattern` collects period, unit steps, slowness, nesting, survival and crookedness.

**The circular planar step** (`HPC/BJ59/CircJoints.lean`, `CircCover.lean`, `CircDescent.lean`,
`CircStd.lean`, `CircStep.lean`). Joint positions of batch 84 read off one finite window of the
lifted pattern; the shear on the universal cover; its descent to a homeomorphism of the plane
through `E N (u, v) = exp (v + iπu/N)`, extended by `0 ↦ 0`; the standard circular chains
(`stdCircLink`, with `stdCircLink_meet_iff`, and `stdCircLink_meet_iff'` for every `N ≠ 0`).
`circ_step` realizes a sweep pattern by tame circular chains.

**Assembly** (`HPC/BJ59/CircPlane.lean`, `HPC/BJ59/BJ59E.lean`). Levels from the standard circular
chain of 16 links, alternately with `exists_sweepPattern` and `circ_step`; `planeDescr :
CircDescr Pt`, hence `bjPlaneExample : BJPlaneExample`, and the classification with Bing–Jones's
third class with no named hypothesis (`ho16_classification₄`, `ho16_classification_plane₄`), with
`dp22_theorem61'`, `dp22_theorem61_iff'`, `hoCircleLike'`.

**Non-vacuity** (`HPC/BJ59/NonVac202.lean`, `NonVac202b.lean`): the sweep lemma on one sweep and on
an interval with two events; `circExp 0` and the failure of `stdCircLink_meet_iff` for `N = 0`; the
case `1 ≤ N ≤ 3`; the hypotheses of `circ_step` for a stuttered identity; and, from a half of the
plane example (a continuous snake-like arc of pseudo-arcs), non-degenerate witnesses for
`BJThm10`, `BJStep`, `bj_theorem8_cor_sub`, `exists_window_set` and `bj_theorem5_window`.

**State of the named inputs.** `BJThm10` (batch 201) and `BJPlaneExample` (this batch) are proved;
the classification of homogeneous plane continua holds with no named hypothesis.

**Certificate for the bound on events** (continuation of batch 202, `HPC/BJ59/NonVac202c.lean`):
`nonvac_sweep_four_events` gives a walk with blocks of length 2, windows of width 1 or 2 moving
one end at a time, alternating directions, and exactly four events in `[0, 10]`, with values
`0, 1, 2, 2, 1, 2, 3, 3, 2, 3, 4` there; it climbs from 0 to 4 without returning from 3 to 1, so
it is not crooked on `[0, 10]`. Every structural hypothesis of `sweep_lemma` holds for it.

**The sweep lemma with three events** (`HPC/BJ59/Sweep3.lean`): `sweep_lemma3`, in the block shape
of `sweep_lemma`, with the commission's bound (no four events in the interval). Case 2b is isolated
as a statement about the windows alone (`sweep_window_case2b`): after the event raising the bottom
to `h + 1`, the top grows exactly once before `k − 1` is reached, the up block after that growth ends
at `k − 1`, and the following down block ends at `h + 1`; each alternative produces a fourth event.
`nonvac_sweep_three_events` applies it to the certificate walk on `[0, 7]` (events `1, 2, 3`).
