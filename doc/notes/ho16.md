## The target

H–O p. 180: every indecomposable non-degenerate homogeneous plane continuum is a pseudo-arc. With
Hagopian's theorem (homogeneous indecomposable plane continua are hereditarily indecomposable,
`hagopian76_theorem1`) and Theorem C (they have span zero, `theoremC`, batch 192), it remains to show
H–O Theorem 1, "if": a hereditarily indecomposable continuum with span zero is snake-like. For plane
continua this is `ho16_theorem1_plane`, from the named `Prop` `HOThm20`.

## The proof of Theorem 1 (H–O p. 211), as formalized

Given `ε > 0`:
1. **Theorem 4** (`ho16_theorem4`, path form) gives `δ`: for every compact `G` with
   `G ⊆ thickening δ X` and every path `γ` on `[0, 1]` that is `δ`-close to `X` in both directions,
   the set `M = {(x, t) ∈ G × (0, 1) : d(x, γ t) < ε/6}` separates `G × {0}` from `G × {1}`. Proof as
   H–O's: otherwise the cut wire theorem gives a continuum in `{d ≥ ε/6}` meeting both end rows; a
   Hausdorff limit of such continua, as `δ → 0`, is a continuum `Z ⊆ X × X` off the diagonal with
   `π₁(Z) ⊆ π₂(Z) = X`, contradicting span zero.
2. **A graph and a map** (`exists_plGraph_approx`). A hereditarily indecomposable plane continuum
   has empty interior (`interior_eq_empty_of_hi`: a closed ball inside it is a continuum, the union of its
   left and right closed halves, which are proper subcontinua). In each square of a fine grid that meets `X`, `X` misses an inner point `c`;
   radial projection from `c` onto the square's frontier maps `X` into the union of the grid edges
   of those squares, a PL graph `G ⊆ thickening δ X`, by a map `f` with `d(f x, x) < δ`. This
   replaces H–O's tree `T` (cited from [31] / [46]; ledger HO.2), and avoids the step
   "It follows that d_H(X, T) < δ" (ledger HO.7).
3. **A path** (`exists_path_approx`): a polygonal path through a fine chain of points of `X`.
4. **Theorem 20** (`HOThm20`, the only open input) gives `h : X → U ∩ (G × [0, 1])`, `U` the open
   set `{d(x, γ t) < ε/6}`, with `d(f x, (h x).1) < ε/6`.
5. The `t`-coordinate of `h` is an `ε`-map onto `[0, 1]`, so `X` is snake-like
   (`isSnakeLike_univ_of_epsMap`).

## Theorem 8 and K–M Lemma 2 (the first piece of `HOThm20`)

`km77_lemma2`: in a hereditarily indecomposable compactum, disjoint closed `A ⊆ U`, `B ⊆ V` admit a
closed cover `X₁ ∪ X₂ ∪ X₃` with `A ⊆ X₁`, `B ⊆ X₃`, `X₁ ∩ X₃ = ∅`, `X₁ ∩ X₂ ⊆ V`, `X₂ ∩ X₃ ⊆ U`.
K–M's proof; their Lemma 1 is replaced by hereditary indecomposability (ledger HO.6).

`ho16_theorem8_core`: H–O's construction in the proof of Theorem 8, (1) ⇒ (2), before lifting to a
fold. The sets `Kᵢ` are `f⁻¹(cl(Gᵢ \ G₂))` (ledger HO.4); the fans are chosen pairwise disjoint
(radius below half the least distance between the finitely many vertices); K–M Lemma 2 gives the
cover. Instead of Tietze extensions on the pieces `cl L′`, the map is
`h = f + (1 − u)·(c − f)`, where `c(x)` is the vertex of the fan containing `f(x)` and `u` is an
Urysohn function equal to `0` on `X₂ ∩ X₃` (resp. `X₁ ∩ X₂`) and `1` off `U₁` (resp. `U₃`); only the
star-shapedness of a fan about its vertex is used.

## Simple folds as sets (batch 194)

H–O build a simple fold as a quotient of `E₁ ⊔ E₂ ⊔ E₃` (pp. 185–186). Here a fold on a PL graph
`G ⊆ E` lives in `E × ℝ`, with projection `φ = Prod.fst` (`FoldDefs.lean`). `IsFoldTriple G G₁ G₂ G₃`
is (F1)–(F3) with "regular" read as "PL graph"; `IsSimpleFoldOf G G₁ G₂ G₃ F₁ F₂ F₃` adds that each
`Fᵢ` is a PL graph on which `Prod.fst` is injective with image `Gᵢ` (F4), and
`φ(F₁ ∩ F₂) = ∂G₁`, `φ(F₂ ∩ F₃) = ∂G₃`, `F₁ ∩ F₃ = ∅` (F5), where `∂A = relFrontier G A =
cl A ∩ cl (G \ A)`. Since the fold `F₁ ∪ F₂ ∪ F₃` is again a PL graph (in `E × ℝ`), the later
induction (Theorem 20) runs over `E, E × ℝ, (E × ℝ) × ℝ, …` with no change of definitions. Folds
exist for every triple (`exists_simpleFold`, `FoldExists.lean`): after a refinement in which
`G₁, G₂, G₃` are unions of edges, every edge is cut at its midpoint, and `F₁, F₂, F₃` are the lifts
of the edges of `G₁, G₂, G₃` with vertex heights `h₁ = 1` on `∂G₁` and `0` elsewhere, `h₂ = 1`,
`h₃ = 1` on `∂G₃` and `2` elsewhere (so the lifts meet exactly over the frontier vertices). Uniqueness up to homeomorphism (H–O p. 186) is not needed and not formalized.

## The PL-graph library (batch 194)

`Subdiv.lean` proves the core subdivision fact: finitely many nondegenerate segments, and a finite
set of points on them, have a common subdivision into pieces any two of which meet at most in a
common endpoint (`Subdiv.exists_common_subdivision`; pieces are cut at the endpoints and the
pairwise intersection endpoints, and each piece is kept once). `PLLib.lean` builds on it:
`isPLGraph_biUnion`, `IsPLGraph.union`, and **refinement** (`IsPLGraph.exists_refinement`: a
decomposition of `G` in which given PL subgraphs `A₁, …, A_k` are unions of edges, other edges meet
them only at endpoints, and a given finite set consists of vertices). From a refined decomposition:
`closure (G \ A)` is the union of the edges not in `A` (`closure_diff_eq_of_refined`, hence
`IsPLGraph.closure_diff`); relative frontiers lie in the vertex set
(`relFrontier_subset_vertices`, `IsPLGraph.relFrontier_finite`); a component is a union of edges
(`connectedComponentIn_eq_biUnion`, `IsPLGraph.connectedComponentIn`), there are finitely many
(`IsPLGraph.finite_components`), and the trace of a PL subgraph on a component is a PL graph
(`IsPLGraph.inter_connectedComponentIn`); `Prod.fst` maps a PL graph on which it is injective onto a
PL graph (`IsPLGraph.image_fst`); a PL graph has no isolated points relative to finite sets
(`IsPLGraph.mem_closure_diff_finite`). Components are open in a PL graph
(`IsPLGraph.comp_mem_nhdsWithin`, `Prop7.lean`).

## Sides, straight sets and stairwells (batch 195)

**Encoding of Definitions 13 and 16.** A stairwell structure `⟨S₁, …, S_k⟩` is a pair of
sequences `T α : ℕ → Set (E × ℝ)`: the sheets are `T 1, …, T k`, and the end-set pieces are
`α 1, …, α (k + 1)`, with H–O's `βᵢ` *defined* as `α (i + 1)`. So the identity "`βᵢ = αᵢ₊₁`" of
(S2) holds by construction, `α₁ = ∅` is `α 1 = ∅` and `β_k = ∅` is `α (k + 1) = ∅`; values of `T`
and `α` outside these ranges are irrelevant. The `αᵢ` are sets of points of `G⋆ = G × [0, 1]`
(each `α (i + 1)` lies in both `𝓔(Sᵢ)` and `𝓔(Sᵢ₊₁)`), and their projections `π(αᵢ)` are what
(S3)–(S5) talk about. A broken structure (`IsBrokenStairwellStr G S k i₀ T α γ P₁ P₂`) uses the
same `T`, `α`, plus a single set `γ` for `γ_{i₀}` and the two pit sheets `P₁`, `P₂`; `1 ≤ i₀ ≤ k`
is explicit. "Neighbourhood `V` of a set `B`" is `V ∈ 𝓝ˢ B` in `E`; for `B = ∅` every set
qualifies, matching H–O's allowance of empty `αᵢ`.

**Generic families** are lists: `IsGeneric G L` for `L : List (Set E)` says that every member is
a finite subset of `G` consisting of ordinary points (`IsOrdinaryPt`: a neighbourhood in `G` which
is the union of two segments meeting only at the point — order 2, independent of the
decomposition, `isOrdinaryPt_iff_order`) and that the list is pairwise disjoint. The family of (S5)
is `(List.range' 2 (k - 1)).map (π '' α ·)`, i.e. `⟨π(α₂), …, π(α_k)⟩`; (S5′) appends
`π(𝓔(P₁))` and `π(γ)`. Relabellings (Proposition 17) are handled by `IsGeneric.perm` and
`IsGeneric.sublist`.

**Straight sets** (`IsStraight G S`): `S ⊆ G⋆` closed, `π` injective on `S`, `π(S)` a PL graph; `S`
itself is not required to be PL. The end set is `endSet G S = S ∩ π⁻¹(∂π(S))`, with `∂` the frontier
relative to `G`. **Folds:** for a fold `F = F₁ ∪ F₂ ∪ F₃ ⊆ E × ℝ`, `φ⋆(q) = (q.1.1, q.2)` is
`phiStar`, and `φ⋆⁻¹(S)` taken inside `F⋆` is `foldPre F S = F⋆ ∩ phiStar⁻¹(S)`. Lemma 18 states
straightness of `foldPre F S` *in `F⋆`* (`IsStraight F ·`), with end sets and the `(∂Fᵢ)⋆` taken in
`F`.

**Sides.** `sideOf G B A` is the closure of the union of the components of `G \ B` meeting `A`
(components are `connectedComponentIn (G \ B) x`); `HasConsCompl G A B` quantifies over the
components `connectedComponentIn (G \ A) x`, `x ∈ G \ A`, with frontiers relative to `G`. The
workhorse is `IsPLGraph.sideOf_char_aux` (`SidesLib.lean`): for `A` a PL subgraph and `B ⊆ ∂A` with
consistent complement, `σ_B(A)` is a PL graph containing `A` with `∂σ_B(A) = B` and
`σ_B(A) ∩ V = A ∩ V` near `B`. Connectedness of `G` is not needed for it. Proposition 10's second
part is proved without H–O's neighbourhood argument: a component `C` of `G \ A` whose closure meets
`B` has `∂C ⊆ B`, hence is a component of `G \ B` missing `A`, hence misses `σ_B(A) = σ_B(A′)`,
hence misses `A′`, and it is then the component of `G \ A′` through any of its points.

## Proposition 19 (batch 196)

**Ordinary points of a fold** (`OrdFold.lean`). `IsSimpleFoldOf.isOrdinaryPt_iff`: if the points of
`∂G₂` are ordinary in `G`, a point `z ∈ F` is ordinary in `F` iff `π(z)` is ordinary in `G`. Being
ordinary is local (`IsOrdinaryPt.of_local`) and transported by `π` on a PL graph on which `π` is
injective, through the order count of batch 195 (`isOrdinaryPt_image_fst_iff`); this handles every
point off `∂F₂`, which has a neighbourhood in `F` inside one sheet. At a glueing point
`z ∈ F₁ ∩ F₂` (or `F₂ ∩ F₃`), `G₂` lies on one side of `π(z)` near it (`one_side_of_relFrontier`), so
each sheet is a single segment near `z` (`exists_single_spoke`) and the two spokes meet only at `z`
(`IsSimpleFoldOf.isOrdinaryPt_of_mem_inter`).

**The full fold** (`P19Ctx.lean` … `Prop19.lean`). The hypotheses are bundled in a structure `P19`
(the broken structure, `γ ≠ ∅`, `𝓔(P₁) ≠ ∅`, and a simple fold with H–O's `G₁ = π(P₁)`,
`G₂ = π(P₂)`, `G₃ = σ_{π(γ)}(π(S_{i₀}))`); the sets of p. 203 are `p19T`, `p19α` (one-`α` encoding,
`βᵢ = α (i+1)`). Straightness and end sets ((19.4)–(19.9)) come from Lemma 18 for the levels
`i ≠ i₀`, `P′₁` and `P′₂`; the level `i₀` is done by hand (`π(S′_{i₀}) = F₁ ∪ F₂ ∪ (F₃ ∩ φ⁻¹(π(S_{i₀})))`,
`P19.straight_T'₀`, `P19.endSet_T'₀`). (S3′) pulls back the neighbourhoods of (S3′) for `S` along `φ`
(`P19.S3'T`, `P19.S3'P`, `P19.S3'γ`); (S5′) is `isOrdinaryPt_iff` plus disjointness of preimages
(`P19.gen'_stair`, `P19.gen'_broken`); (S6′) is `α′_{i₀+1} ⊆ int(F₃)⋆`. (S4′) (`Prop19D.lean`): a
component `D` of `F \ π(S′ᵢ)` maps into a component `D̃` of `G \ π(Sᵢ)` and `∂D` lies over `∂D̃`
(`relFrontier_over`); at level `i₀ + 1` the frontier is split between `F₃` and `F₁ ∪ F₂` by
Proposition 10 (second part, used contrapositively as on p. 208) and Lemma 6 (6) (`P19.split_i₀1`);
for `P′₂` the case split `∂D = ∅` / `∂D ≠ ∅` replaces H–O's false sentence (`P19.split_P'₂`; the
certificate is `ho16_p208_false`, `LEDGER.md` HO.25).

**The connected fold** (`Prop19Conn.lean`). The first part of Proposition 7 gives a component `C`
of `F` with `φ(C) = G`; it meets `∂F₃ = F₂ ∩ F₃` (`P19.comp_meets_F₂₃`, from the sides of Lemma 6
(6)), so every set of the structure meets `C⋆`, and Observations 13 and 16 for components restrict
the structure to `C` (`ho16_prop19`). H–O's component `K` of p. 209 is not needed. The statement is
also recorded as a named `Prop`, `HOProp19`, proved by `hoProp19_holds`.

## Theorem 20 from Theorem 15 (batch 196)

`ho16_lift_aux` (`Thm20.lean`) is a strong induction on a natural number `n`, quantified over the
ambient space `E : Type` (each fold passes from `E` to `E × ℝ`): for a stairwell structure of odd
height `k` with `(k+1)² ≤ n`, or a broken one of odd height `k` with a pit at `i₀` and
`(k+1)² + (k+1−i₀) ≤ n`, on a connected PL graph `G`, every map `f : X → G` of a hereditarily
indecomposable compactum is `ε`-approximated by `π ∘ h` with `h : X → S`. Height 1: the section of
`ho16_height_one`. Height `k ≥ 3`: Proposition 17 gives a broken structure of height `k − 2` with a
pit at level 1. Broken, `γ = ∅` or `𝓔(P₁) = ∅`: the remarks of p. 201 (`ho16_rem17_gamma`,
`ho16_rem17_P1`) give a stairwell structure on a subset of `S`. Broken otherwise: Proposition 19
gives a connected fold `F` and `S′ ⊆ φ⋆⁻¹(S)`; Theorem 8 for folds gives `g : X → F` within `ε/2`;
the induction hypothesis in `E × ℝ` gives `h′ : X → S′` within `ε/2`, and `h = φ⋆ ∘ h′`. The
measure drops at each step and oddness is preserved. `ho16_lift_stairwell` is the stairwell case.

`ho16_theorem20_of_thm15` reduces to connected `G` (components of a PL graph are finitely many and
clopen; `M ∩ C⋆` separates in `C⋆`; the maps on the clopen sets `f⁻¹(C)` are glued), applies
`HOThm15` on each component and `ho16_lift_stairwell` to the result.

## Ladders and Theorem 15 (batch 197)

**Ladders** (`LadderDefs.lean`). `IsLadder G S y ev` says: `S` is an oriented decomposition of
`G` into segments; at each vertex `v` there is a list `y v` of heights in `(0, 1)` of odd length
`j(v)`; on each edge `s` either `ev s = none` and `j(s.1) = j(s.2)`, or `ev s = some (m, λ, h)`: a
side wedge at `s.2`, with `j(s.2) = j(s.1) + 2`, places `m`, `m + 1` at `s.2`, tip over parameter
`λ` at height `h`. The sheet `ladderSheet S y ev i` is the union over edges of `ladderPiece`: the
segment from `(s.1, yᵢ(s.1))` to `(s.2, yᵢ(s.2))` without event, and on a wedge edge the lines
`Lᵢ` from `(s.1, yᵢ(s.1))` to `(s.2, yᵢ₊₂(s.2))` (`i ≥ m`) cut into the three overlapping arcs of a
zig-zag over `[0, rᵢ]`, `[lᵢ, rᵢ]`, `[lᵢ, 1]`, going into sheets `i`, `i + 1`, `i + 2`, plus the two
wedge halves from the tip. The zig-zag parameters `lᵢ < rᵢ` (`zzL`, `zzR`) are ordered
`0 < l_j < r_j < … < l_m < r_m < λ` (`zz_lr`, `zz_lam`), so over an edge the pieces of one sheet
lie over pairwise disjoint parameter intervals (`eI_disj`, `LadderPiece.lean`).

**The stairwell structure** (`LadderStair.lean`, `LadderStair2.lean`; `ho16_stairwell_of_ladder`).
The end sets are given explicitly (`ladderAlphaSet`: the turning points of the zig-zags and the
tips), since overlapping sheets make H–O's `β_i = S_i ∩ S_{i+1}` unavailable. (S1): `π` is injective
on each sheet (`injOn_fst_ladderSheet`) and `π(Sᵢ)` is a PL graph (`isPLGraph_image_fst_ladderSheet`);
the sheets are straight (`isStraight_ladderSheet`). (S2): a vertex is interior to `π(Sᵢ)` iff
`i ≤ j(v)`, so the frontier of `π(Sᵢ)` is the set of parameters where pieces stop inside edges,
which is `π(αᵢ ∪ αᵢ₊₁)` (`relFrontier_image_fst_ladderSheet`, `endSet_ladderSheet`). (S3): at each
end point the two sheets leave in the same direction (`ladder_S3`). (S4): a component of
`G \ π(Sᵢ)` is either a gap inside an edge (`gapA`, `gapB`) or contains vertices with `j(v) < i`
only; in the second case every frontier point sits at the first piece of sheet `i` on a wedge edge
leaving such a vertex, and it is in `β_i` or `α_i` according to `i − j(v) ∈ {1, 2}`; since all
`j(v)` are odd, the label depends only on the parity of `i` (`vertex_label`,
`relFrontier_comp_ladder`, `ladder_S4`). This is H–O's parity argument of p. 198. (S5): all end
points lie over interior points of edges, which are ordinary, at pairwise different parameters
(`ladder_S5`). Connectedness of `G` is not used; only `S ≠ ∅` (so that the height is attained).

**A ladder near a separator** (`LadderSep.lean`, `LadderSubdiv.lean`, `LadderSign.lean`,
`LadderExists.lean`; `exists_ladder`, replacing Claim 15.1). From the open sets `O₀`, `O₁` of the
separation, `K = G⋆ \ (U ∩ E × (0, 1))` splits into disjoint compact sets `K₀ ∋ G × {0}` and
`K₁ ∋ G × {1}`, and `u = infDist · K₀ − infDist · K₁` is 2-Lipschitz, `≤ 0` at the bottom and
`> 0` at the top; with `δ` small, any point within `δ` of a point where `u ≤ 0` and of a point
where `u > 0` lies in `U ∩ G × (0, 1)` (`exists_sign_fn`; the Lipschitz bound replaces uniform
continuity). Subdivide `G` into edges shorter than `δ` (`subdiv`) and take levels `t_q = q/N`. The
list at a point with sign sequence `σ` is the list of midpoints `(t_q + t_{q+1})/2` of the sign
changes of `σ` (`sgnList`, `chg`; odd length by `odd_length_chg`). Each edge is cut uniformly into
`N − 1` pieces, the `k`-th point carrying the signs of `s.2` at levels `≤ k` and of `s.1` above, so
that consecutive points differ at one level only; one flip moves one curve by one level, or creates
two curves (a wedge at the far end, read off the two lists by `wedgeEv`, tip at `λ = ½`, height
`t_q`), or removes two (the piece is oriented backwards, `lo`) (`chg_flip`, `chgPair_cases`). Every
point of a sheet then lies within `δ` of two grid corners with opposite signs
(`heightOK_of_mem_piece`, `LData.sheet_corners`), hence in `U ∩ G × (0, 1)`.
